# M2.5-08 lakes, spill levels and shorelines

**Status:** landed 2026-11-01. The filled hydrology surface now yields lake
records with flat surfaces, spill cells, shorelines and lake beds.

## Acceptance

| Requirement | Result |
|---|---|
| Lake extraction | 8-connected flat components above their bed |
| Spill levels | the surface is the component's filled elevation; the spill is the rim cell the lake's flow forest drains into |
| Shoreline | every non-lake cell 8-adjacent to the water, row-major |
| Underwater terrain | `max_depth` and per-cell beds stay in the hydrology grid; the tile keeps the original heights |
| No leaking basins | every neighbour of a lake cell has `filled >= surface`; the spill leaves the level without re-entering |
| Tests | `cargo test -p voxel-worldgen lakes` (11 integration + 1 unit) |

## Model

### Components are flat and 8-connected

A lake cell is a cell whose filled elevation is above its stored elevation.
The component is **8-connected** — diagonally touching water is one body — and
restricted to a **single filled elevation**. Adjacent wet cells at different
elevations are two water bodies (one spills into the other), and treating them
as one would give a lake a stepped surface.

### Surface and spill

Priority-flood fills a depression to one level: the filled elevation `S` is the
minimum, over escape paths, of the highest bed on the path. That is the lake's
**surface**. The **spill** is not simply the lowest rim cell: several rim cells
can sit at `S`, and some are inlets whose flow crosses the flat surface. The
extractor takes the rim cells that lake cells actually drain into (the roots of
the lake's flow forest) and follows each one's flow until the level drops or the
map outlet, rejecting any root that re-enters the lake. The remaining root is
the outlet.

Because the flood pops in non-decreasing order, no lake cell can border a lower
filled cell, and the rim that discovered the lake sits exactly at `S`. That is
the **no leaking basins** guarantee, checked on every extracted lake.

### Shoreline

`shoreline` is every non-lake cell 8-adjacent to the component, sorted
row-major. For a lake without islands it is one ring; an island adds a second
ring. The tests require that every shoreline cell touches the lake and at least
one other shoreline cell — no isolated points, so the ring has no gaps.

### Filter

`LakeConfig::min_cells` defaults to **8 cells** (about a 360 m pond). The
valley detail produces millions of single-cell puddles; keeping them would
dwarf the real lakes (and carve thousands of pointless holes). `lake_cells()`
still reports the unfiltered count so the loss is visible.

## Full-world run

```sh
cargo run -p voxel-worldgen --release -- lakes --seed 1
```

Reference machine (Ryzen 5 7600), release, 8,000 × 8,000 cells:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| Lake cells (unfiltered) | 2,758,410 (4.31%) |
| Lakes with `min_cells = 8` | 4,003 |
| Kept area | 2,722,770 cells (98.7% of lake cells) |
| Largest lake | 129,095 cells (≈ 2,116 km²) |
| Largest lake surface | 28,540 units (7,135 m) |
| Deepest lake | 1,221 units (305 m) |
| Heightfield + hydrology | 13.0 s + 11.6 s |
| Extraction | 0.22 s |
| Peak RSS | 1.05 GiB |
| Lake hash | `8bd50ef2880a202d` |

M2.5-07 found 21,281 river mouths ending at lakes; only 4,003 lakes survive the
size filter here, because a river ends at any lake at least one metre deep.
Linking river mouths to lake records is a store-level change (M2.5-27).

## Tests

- Hand-checked bowl: 9 cells, surface 3, depth 2, spill `(4, 2)`, the whole
  5 × 5 frame is shoreline, no leaks, the spill drains off the map.
- Two basins with different spill levels (ridge spill at 5, border spill at 3)
  are two lakes, not one.
- Random fields (six seeds): invariants on every lake.
- Uniform surface and bed consistency; area filter; determinism.
- A 256 × 256 window of the generated world, so the same invariants run on real
  terrain.
