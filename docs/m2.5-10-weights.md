# M2.5-10 biome weights

**Status:** landed 2026-11-01. The 18 biome centres now produce a softmax weight
vector that sums to one and blends over 300–500 m.

## Acceptance

| Requirement | Result |
|---|---|
| Softmax over 18 centres | `BiomeWeights::at(&Axes)`, five axes |
| Axes: climate, erosion, continentalness | continentalness, temperature, humidity, erosion and a depth term |
| Weights sum to 1 | exactly `ONE` on every sampled axis; the rounding residual is absorbed by the largest weight |
| Transition width 300–500 m | **348 m** on the desert/savanna transect at the design's edge gradient |
| No argmax curve | at every dominance flip both top weights are between 0.25 and 0.75 |
| Blend shape parameters before evaluation | `BiomeWeights::blend` |

Verify with `cargo test -p voxel-worldgen weights` (10 integration tests plus 2
unit tests).

## Model

### Axes

| Axis | Source | Remap |
|---|---|---|
| continentalness | macro field, `[-1, 1]` | `(c + 1) / 2` |
| temperature | climate channel `u8` | `× ONE / 255` |
| humidity | climate channel `u8` | `× ONE / 255` |
| erosion | macro field, `[0, 1]` | as-is |
| depth | 0 at the surface, 1 deep | M2.5-15 supplies it |

`Axes::surface(c, temperature, humidity, erosion)` is the surface constructor;
M2.5-15 adds depth. At depth 0 the underground biomes carry only trace weight;
at depth 1 the nearest underground centre wins (tested for `fungal_deep`).

### Centres

The 18 keys follow the design: 11 land, 3 ocean, 3 underground and the rare
geothermal. The numeric centres are placeholders — M2.5-11 moves them into the
biome RON and validates that every material key resolves. Keeping the keys
stable now means the weight tests already pin the full set.

| Class | Count | Keys |
|---|---:|---|
| Land | 11 | beach, plains, forest, taiga, swamp, hills, mountains, alpine, desert, savanna, tundra |
| Ocean | 3 | shelf, deep, trench |
| Underground | 3 | aquifer, fungal_deep, cavern |
| Rare | 1 | geothermal |

### Softmax

```text
score_i = exp(-d_i² / T)
weight_i = round(score_i · ONE / Σ score)
```

`T = 0.03` in squared normalised units. The exponential is a **compile-time
table** of `exp(-x)` for `x ∈ [0, 8]` in 1/16 steps, built by a 48-term Taylor
series in `const fn` and forced monotone; the table is accurate to one or two
units of Q16.16. No `libm`, no float, no platform-dependent rounding.

Distances beyond the table (far biomes) clamp to `exp(-8) ≈ 0.0003`, so their
weight is a trace rather than zero and the normalisation stays smooth.

### Transition width

The weight function is smooth; how wide the blend is in metres depends on how
fast the axes move. The design's assumption is that the weight field varies
over 300–500 m, and the test states the model explicitly:

> The humidity axis moves from 0.05 to 0.50 over 600 m — one centre gap of
> 0.45 over 600 m, a steep biome-edge gradient.

On that transect the desert weight falls from 0.9 to 0.1 over **348 m**. The
band where both top weights exceed 0.1 is 342 m. Both are inside 300–500.

This is the number M2.5-13 should use when it dithers materials at 1 m: the
weight band is a few hundred metres, not a pixel-wide edge.

### No argmax curve

Along the same transect, whenever the dominant biome changes, the outgoing and
incoming top weights are both between 0.25 and 0.75 — the crossover happens in
the middle of a genuine blend, not at a hard threshold. A 2D sweep at 4 m
sampling (0.0025 axis steps) moves no weight by more than 5% per step, so a
material dither has a continuum to work with.

### Blending shape parameters

`BiomeWeights::blend(&[i32; 18])` returns the weighted parameter in Q16.16.
The design rule is to blend shape parameters, not finished heights:

```text
ok:   height = surface_height(blend(c), blend(e))
bad:  height = blend(surface_height(c_i, e_i))   // straight line through a ridge
```

M2.5-12 uses the first form for surface rules; the test pins the arithmetic
(weighted constants and a weighted ramp).

## Determinism

The weights are a pure function of the five axes. The exponential table and all
interpolation are integer, and the rounding residual is assigned to the largest
weight deterministically. Two runs give bit-identical vectors.

## Tests

- Sum exactly one over a 5 × 5 × 6 × 5 axis grid; no negative weight; always a
  dominant biome.
- Every biome is dominant at its own centre (with its own depth).
- Transition width 300–500 m on the stated edge transect.
- No hard argmax switch; map continuity at 4 m steps.
- Shape-parameter blending (constant and ramp).
- Underground biomes unreachable at the surface, reachable at depth.
- Surface remapping; ocean depth bands (trench, shelf).
- Determinism.
