# M2.5-12 surface rules

**Status:** landed 2026-11-01. `crate::surface` turns a biome palette plus a
local terrain sample into the top few blocks of a column, with the design's
cliff, underwater, altitude, beach and soil precedence.

## Acceptance

| Requirement | Result |
|---|---|
| 3–6 block soil default | standard land biomes (`plains`, `forest`, `hills`, `taiga`) pick 3–6 blocks from their `soil_depth` range; the hash varies the depth inside it |
| Biome/altitude override | dry, cold, coastal and mountain biomes declare shorter ranges in `data/biomes.ron`; rock above 2,400 m and snow above 3,200 m override every palette |
| Slope rule | a slope of 45° or more exposes the palette's stone, with zero soil, above and below water |
| Underwater rule | water-lain material to 2 blocks: the ocean palette's own floor, or a land biome's beach material for lake and river beds |
| Cliffs expose stone | `SurfaceKind::Cliff` has `top == filler == stone == palette.stone` and `soil_depth == 0` |
| Beaches ring water | a beach needs `shore == true`, at most 16 m above sea level and a slope under 14°; the same low column without a shore stays soil |
| Every key comes from M2.5-30 | `tests/surface.rs` resolves every emitted top, filler and stone key through the committed `blocks.ron`/`materials.gen.ron`; every palette role reaches the output |
| Tests | `cargo test -p voxel-worldgen surface` — 9 unit + 5 manifest tests |

## Precedence

```text
cliff  →  underwater  →  snow  →  rock  →  beach  →  soil
```

The order is deliberate:

1. **Cliff** — a steep face is bedrock even on a snow peak or an underwater
   canyon wall. The palette's `stone` is used, so a desert cliff is sandstone.
2. **Underwater** — at or below sea level the surface is depositional. Ocean
   biomes use their own `top`/`filler` (shelf sand, deep floor, trench floor);
   a land column under a lake or river uses its `beach` material, so a drowned
   plain is sand/gravel/clay rather than grass.
3. **Snow** — at 3,200 m above sea level (`SNOW_LINE`) the top is
   `voxel:snow` over `voxel:stone`, regardless of biome.
4. **Rock** — at 2,400 m above sea level (`ROCK_LINE`) the top is
   `voxel:stone`; this is what makes a desert mountain lose its red rock near
   the peak. The universal stone is used, not the palette's.
5. **Beach** — low, gentle and on a shore. The shore flag is what makes the
   ring follow water instead of every low inland basin.
6. **Soil** — the biome palette and the `soil_depth` range.

## API

```rust
pub struct SurfaceSample {
    pub height: i32,   // Q16.16 metres above the world floor
    pub slope: i32,    // Q16.16 rise over run; ONE is 45°
    pub shore: bool,   // within the shore band of ocean/lake/river
    pub hash: u64,     // per-column hash for the soil depth
}

pub fn column(biome: &BiomeDef, sample: SurfaceSample) -> SurfaceColumn<'_>;
```

`SurfaceColumn` carries `top`, `filler`, `stone`, `soil_depth` and a
`SurfaceKind`. `soil_depth` counts the non-stone cover including the top
block, so `filler_depth() == soil_depth - 1` and `soil_depth == 0` means bare
stone. The keys borrow the biome definition, so a plan allocates nothing; the
chunk pipeline resolves them to `MaterialId` once per column.

`column_for(index, sample)` is the catalogue-index convenience, and
`soil_depth(biome, hash)` is the deterministic range picker.

## Soil depths

| Kind | Total cover | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| Soil | 3–6 for the standard land biomes | biome `soil_depth` range, hash chooses inside |
| Beach | 3 | fixed [`BEACH_SOIL_DEPTH`] |
| Seabed | 2 | fixed [`SEABED_SOIL_DEPTH`] |
| Cliff | 0 | stone exposed |
| Rock | 0 | stone exposed |
| Snow | 1 | one snow block over rock |

The biome ranges are the override half of the rule. Standard land biomes sit
at 3–6; `hills` and `taiga` at 3–5; `desert`, `swamp` and `savanna` at 2–4/5;
`tundra`, `beach` and `mountains` at 1–3; `alpine` at 1–2. The hash varies the
depth inside the declared range, so a plains column is not always the same
thickness and the same column always regenerates the same way.

## Constants

| Constant | Value | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `BEACH_SOIL_DEPTH` | 3 | beach sand including the top |
| `SEABED_SOIL_DEPTH` | 2 | water-lain cover including the top |
| `DEFAULT_SOIL_MIN` / `DEFAULT_SOIL_MAX` | 3 / 6 | the standard land default |
| `BEACH_BAND` | 16 m | shore height above sea level |
| `BEACH_MAX_SLOPE` | 0.25 (14°) | steepest beach |
| `CLIFF_SLOPE` | 1.0 (45°) | bedrock exposed |
| `ROCK_LINE` | 2,400 m above sea | universal rock |
| `SNOW_LINE` | 3,200 m above sea | universal snow |
| `HARD_STONE` / `SNOW` | `voxel:stone` / `voxel:snow` | canonical override keys |

## Tests

Unit (`src/surface.rs`):

- every biome's soil uses its own palette and a depth inside its range;
- the hash covers the declared range;
- the standard land default is 3–6, and the override biomes really are lower;
- every biome exposes its stone on a 45° slope, above and under water;
- a beach needs a shore, the band and a gentle slope; the band is inclusive at
  16 m and excludes 17 m;
- every biome's underwater column uses the ocean floor or its beach material;
- snow and rock override every palette, including the desert's red sand;
- repeated calls return the same column.

Manifest (`tests/surface.rs`):

- every emitted top/filler/stone key resolves in the frozen block registry;
- every biome's top, filler, stone and beach keys reach the output somewhere;
- `voxel:stone` and `voxel:snow` resolve;
- `column_for` matches `column`;
- ocean biomes submerged use their own palette rather than a land beach.
